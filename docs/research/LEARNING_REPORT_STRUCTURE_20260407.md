## C) Key Findings Summary

### C.1) The Standard Wall Street Report Structure (Consensus Across Sources)

After analyzing reports and guidelines from Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JP Morgan, CFA Institute, and multiple professional training organizations, the following represents the **consensus institutional report structure**:

#### Tier 1: "Initiating Coverage" Report (50-100+ pages)

| # | Section | Purpose | Typical Length |
|---|---------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | **Cover Page / Header Block** | Ticker, price, target, rating, key metrics snapshot, analyst info | 1 page |
| 2 | **Investment Recommendation & Target Price** | Buy/Sell/Hold with 12-month price target, upside/downside % | 0.5 page |
| 3 | **Executive Summary / Investment Thesis** | 2-3 key arguments for the recommendation, key catalysts | 1-2 pages |
| 4 | **Company Description & Business Model** | What the company does, how it makes money, key segments | 3-5 pages |
| 5 | **Industry Overview & Competitive Positioning** | TAM, market share, Porter's Five Forces, competitive moat analysis | 5-10 pages |
| 6 | **Management Assessment** | CEO/CFO track record, capital allocation history, insider ownership, governance | 2-3 pages |
| 7 | **Financial Analysis** | Historical income statement, balance sheet, cash flow; ratio analysis | 5-8 pages |
| 8 | **Financial Projections / Earnings Model** | Quarterly and annual P&L, BS, CF forecasts (typically 2-3 years out) | 3-5 pages |
| 9 | **Valuation** | DCF, comparable company multiples, SOTP if applicable; sensitivity tables | 5-8 pages |
| 10 | **Catalysts & Upcoming Events** | Earnings dates, product launches, regulatory milestones, M&A potential | 1-2 pages |
| 11 | **Risk Factors** | Comprehensive risk taxonomy: operational, financial, regulatory, macro, ESG | 2-4 pages |
| 12 | **ESG Assessment** | Environmental, social, governance scoring and material risks | 1-3 pages |
| 13 | **Appendix: Detailed Financial Exhibits** | Full 3-statement model tables, segment breakdowns, historical data | 5-10 pages |
| 14 | **Disclosures, Disclaimers & Analyst Certification** | Regulatory requirements, conflicts of interest, rating definitions | 2-5 pages |

#### Tier 2: "Company Update / Note" Report (1-5 pages)

| # | Section | Purpose |
|---|---------|---------|
| 1 | **Header with Rating & Target** | Quick-reference data block |
| 2 | **Key Takeaway** | 1-2 sentence summary of the update |
| 3 | **Analysis** | What changed and why it matters |
| 4 | **Updated Estimates Table** | Revised EPS/revenue forecasts vs prior |
| 5 | **Valuation Impact** | How the target price changes |
| 6 | **Risks** | Any new/modified risks |
| 7 | **Disclosures** | Regulatory boilerplate |

### C.2) Rating Systems Across Banks

| Bank | System | Scale |
|------|--------|-------|
| Goldman Sachs | Buy / Neutral / Sell + **Conviction List** (top picks) | 3-tier + CL |
| Morgan Stanley | Overweight / Equal-Weight / Underweight | 3-tier relative |
| JP Morgan | Overweight / Neutral / Underweight | 3-tier relative |
| Bank of America | Buy / Neutral / Underperform | 3-tier |
| UBS | Buy / Neutral / Sell | 3-tier |

**Key observation:** All major banks use a **3-tier system**, not 5-tier. The CFA Institute explicitly notes that 3-tier systems reduce ambiguity. Goldman's "Conviction List" adds a fourth tier for highest-conviction calls without adding a fourth rating label.

### C.3) Valuation Methodology Standards

Banks typically present **multiple valuation approaches** side by side:

1. **DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)** -- The primary intrinsic valuation
   - Always includes explicit WACC assumption (typically 7-11%)
   - Always includes terminal growth rate (typically 2-3%)
   - Always presented with a **sensitivity table** (WACC vs. growth rate grid)
   - Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan both use sensitivity tables as a standard exhibit

2. **Comparable Company Analysis ("Comps")** -- Relative valuation
   - P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/S, P/FCF multiples
   - Compared against defined peer group (typically 5-10 companies)
   - Forward multiples preferred over trailing

3. **Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP)** -- For multi-segment companies
   - Each segment valued independently using appropriate methodology
   - Net debt subtracted from aggregate TEV to get equity value
   - Used prominently for companies like Amazon (e-commerce + AWS + ads), Alphabet (Search + Cloud + Waymo), Meta (FoA + Reality Labs)

4. **Probability-Weighted Scenarios** -- For high-uncertainty names
   - Bull/Base/Bear cases with explicit probability weights
   - Each scenario has its own assumptions and resulting price
   - Weighted average gives the target

**Key insight:** The industry standard is to present **at least two** valuation methods and explain any discrepancies between them. A single-method valuation is considered insufficient by CFA Institute standards.

### C.4) How Risk Analysis is Structured

Institutional risk sections follow a **taxonomy-based approach**:

| Risk Category | Examples |
|---------------|----------|
| **Operational Risk** | Execution failure, supply chain disruption, key-person dependency |
| **Financial Risk** | Leverage, liquidity, currency exposure, interest rate sensitivity |
| **Competitive Risk** | Market share loss, pricing pressure, disruptive entrants |
| **Regulatory Risk** | Antitrust, export controls, data privacy, tax policy changes |
| **Macro/Geopolitical Risk** | Recession, trade wars, commodity prices, geopolitical conflicts |
| **Technological Risk** | Obsolescence, cybersecurity, platform migration |
| **ESG Risk** | Environmental liability, labor practices, governance failures |
| **Valuation Risk** | Multiple compression, earnings miss, sentiment shift |

Each risk is typically presented with:
- **Description** of the risk
- **Probability assessment** (qualitative or quantitative)
- **Impact magnitude** if triggered
- **Mitigants** or offsets

### C.5) Data Tables and Charts Usage

Institutional reports are **heavily table-driven**. Standard exhibits include:

1. **Key Metrics Summary Table** (always on page 1)
   - Stock price, target price, market cap, EV, shares outstanding
   - P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/S, dividend yield, beta
   - 52-week high/low

2. **Income Statement Summary** (historical + projected)
   - Typically 3 years historical + 2-3 years projected
   - Revenue, EBITDA, EBIT, Net Income, EPS
   - Quarterly breakdowns for the current and next fiscal year

3. **Segment Revenue Breakdown** (historical + projected)

4. **Comparable Company Table**
   - Full peer group with key valuation and profitability multiples

5. **DCF Sensitivity Table**
   - 2D grid: WACC (rows) vs. terminal growth rate (columns)
   - Center cell = base case implied share price

6. **Scenario Analysis Table**
   - Bull/Base/Bear with key assumptions and resulting prices

7. **Share Price Chart** with key events annotated

Charts are typically: revenue bridge charts, margin trend lines, market share pie charts, stock price with technical levels, and valuation multiple history charts.

### C.6) Management Assessment -- A Missing Section

This is one of the most significant findings. **Every institutional equity research report includes a management assessment section.** This typically covers:

- CEO and CFO tenure and track record
- Capital allocation history (returns on invested capital, M&A track record, buyback effectiveness)
- Insider ownership levels and recent transactions
- Management guidance credibility (history of beating/missing guidance)
- Board composition and governance quality
- Executive compensation alignment with shareholder interests

### C.7) Catalysts Section -- Another Missing Element

Institutional reports always include a **catalysts section** identifying specific upcoming events that could move the stock:

- Next earnings date and key metrics to watch
- Product launches or technology milestones
- Regulatory decisions pending
- M&A activity potential
- Share buyback authorization remaining
- Conference presentations or investor days

### C.8) Financial Projection Tables -- The Model Exhibit

This is arguably the single biggest differentiator between institutional and retail research. Sell-side reports include **detailed financial model exhibits**:

- Full income statement projections (quarterly for current year, annual for 2-3 years)
- Balance sheet projections
- Cash flow statement projections
- Per-share data (EPS, CFPS, BPS, DPS)
- Key ratios projected forward (margins, returns, leverage)
- Revenue build-up by segment with driver assumptions

### C.9) Source Attribution Standards

Institutional reports use these attribution conventions:
- **Company filings** are cited as "Company data" or specific filing (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K)
- **Third-party data** is cited as "Source: FactSet", "Source: Bloomberg", etc.
- **Analyst estimates** are clearly marked as "[Firm] estimates" or "Our estimates"
- **Industry data** cites the specific research firm (Gartner, IDC, McKinsey, etc.)
- **Every table and chart** has a source line at the bottom
- **Consensus estimates** cite the aggregator (LSEG, FactSet, Bloomberg)

### D.4) Sections to Preserve Unchanged (Our Differentiators)

These sections have **no institutional equivalent** and are competitive advantages:

1. **The Provocative Opening** -- No Wall Street report opens with "A $1.5 Trillion Faith-Based Financial Instrument." This is what makes readers click and share. Goldman opens with a data block. We open with a hook. Keep it.

2. **Historical Context** -- The NVIDIA vs. Cisco 2000 comparison is genuinely insightful analysis that most sell-side reports skip because it risks offending management relationships. We have no such constraint. Keep it.

3. **Brutal AI Verdict** -- The editorial opinion section with personality is entirely unique. Institutional reports must remain neutral in tone due to regulatory and client relationship constraints. We don't. This is our competitive moat.

4. **BAAF Scoring System** -- The 6-axis proprietary scoring (Growth, Profitability, Moat, Valuation, Risk, Momentum) is a genuine analytical framework that adds structure to the verdict. It's similar to how some boutique research shops use proprietary scoring, but the specific axes and weightings are uniquely ours.

## F) Remaining Questions / Areas for Further Study

### F.1) Unresolved Questions

1. **Word Count vs. Depth Trade-off:** Institutional initiating coverage reports are 50-100 pages. DHLM Deep Dives are ~3,200 words (~6-7 pages). Adding all recommended sections would push reports to ~5,000-6,000 words (~10-12 pages). Is this acceptable for the DHLM audience, or should we create a "Summary" version and a "Full Report" version?

2. **Financial Model Depth:** Should DHLM reports include full 3-statement projections (income statement + balance sheet + cash flow), or is an abbreviated earnings projection table sufficient? Full models require significantly more data work per report.

3. **SOTP Applicability:** For conglomerates like Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta, should DHLM reports include SOTP valuations? This would significantly differentiate our analysis but requires segment-level modeling.

4. **ESG Integration Level:** How much ESG content is appropriate for the DHLM audience? Options range from (a) a one-line governance note, to (b) a full ESG sub-section with MSCI ratings, to (c) integrating ESG factors directly into the BAAF scoring axes.

5. **Quarterly Update Format:** Should DHLM introduce a shorter "Company Update" format (1-2 pages) for post-earnings or post-event updates, separate from the full Deep Dive? This is standard practice at every sell-side bank and would increase publishing frequency.

### F.2) Areas for Further Study

1. **Buy-Side vs. Sell-Side Differences:** This research focused on sell-side (investment bank) reports. Buy-side (hedge fund) research is more opinionated, shorter, and focused on variant perception. DHLM's editorial voice is actually closer to buy-side style. A study of buy-side research formats (e.g., Pershing Square, Muddy Waters, Citron Research) could yield additional insights.

2. **Alternative Data Integration:** Modern equity research increasingly uses alternative data (satellite imagery, web scraping, app download data, credit card data). Study how these data sources are presented and attributed in research reports.

3. **Interactive Report Elements:** Can the web-based format of DHLM reports leverage interactivity that PDF-based institutional reports cannot? For example: interactive DCF sliders, clickable peer comparison filters, real-time price updates in the header data block.

4. **Rating System Formalization:** Should the BAAF scoring system be mapped to a formal 3-tier recommendation system (Buy/Hold/Sell or equivalent)? Currently, BAAF scores imply a recommendation (83 = B+ = generally positive) but don't make it explicit. Institutional convention demands an explicit recommendation.

5. **Regulatory Considerations:** As DHLM Studio grows, study whether any regulatory requirements (SEC, FINRA, MiFID II) would apply to research publications. Currently operating as editorial/educational content, but if the reports gain significant distribution, regulatory classification could change.

6. **Backtesting BAAF Scores:** Study whether historical BAAF scores have predictive power. If BAAF 80+ stocks outperform BAAF 40- stocks over 12 months, that data would be extremely powerful for credibility. This requires 6-12 months of published scores to test.

## Summary

DHLM Studio's Deep Dive reports are already stronger than 90% of retail-facing stock analysis content. The provocative editorial voice, BAAF scoring system, historical context sections, and probability-weighted valuation scenarios are genuine differentiators that no institutional report replicates.

The gaps versus institutional standards are primarily in **quantitative exhibit density** (sensitivity tables, projection tables, comps tables with more metrics) and **structural completeness** (management assessment, catalysts, ESG, formal header data block). These are additive improvements -- they don't require changing what already works.

The single highest-impact, lowest-effort improvement is the **Header Data Block**. Adding a structured metrics table at the top of every report immediately signals institutional quality to readers before they even begin the narrative. The second-highest impact improvement is the **Forward Earnings Projection Table**, which transforms the financial analysis from purely backward-looking to forward-looking -- which is what investors actually need.
