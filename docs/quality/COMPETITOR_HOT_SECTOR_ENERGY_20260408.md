## 3. Where DHLM Studio Matched or Exceeded the Peers

### 3.1 Scenario Table Innovation

The DHLM Studio scenario table (3 oil price scenarios x 3 representative stocks) is more reader-friendly than any of the peer formats. Bloomberg and Goldman both produce DCF-based price targets but they bury them in tables that retail readers cannot parse. Doomberg and Seeking Alpha do not produce scenario tables at all.

**Verdict:** Keep this format. Make it standard for every Hot Sector report.

### 3.2 Historical Parallel Table

The DHLM Studio 4-event historical comparison (1973, 1990, 2022, 2026) is more complete than the Goldman Sachs 2-event version (1973, 1990) and significantly more rigorous than the no-table format used by Bloomberg, Doomberg, and Seeking Alpha. Including the 2022 Russia-Ukraine reference is critical because it is the most recent oil shock and the most directly comparable structurally.

**Verdict:** Keep this format. The 4-event template is the new standard.

### 3.3 Bull AND Bear Case Per Pick

Only Goldman Sachs offered both bull and bear cases for individual stocks, and even Goldman's bear paragraphs were noticeably shorter than the bull paragraphs. DHLM Studio gave equal length and equal force to both sides for all 5 picks, including the OXY pick that was deliberately framed as "high-beta trade, not investment."

This is a structural advantage. Retail readers do not get balanced analysis from Seeking Alpha (mostly bull-biased) or sell-side research (career risk in publishing strong sells). DHLM Studio's editorial independence is a real differentiator.

**Verdict:** This is the moat. Protect it. Every premium report must enforce equal-length bull and bear treatment per stock.

### 3.4 Naming the Tactical Top Pick

Three of the four peers (Bloomberg, Doomberg) refused to name a single conviction call. Goldman named XOM, Seeking Alpha named VLO. DHLM Studio named MPC and explained the asymmetry argument explicitly.

**Verdict:** Always name the pick. The free reader can read 5 sets of analysis. The paid reader is paying for the call.

## 4. Where the Peers Had Real Advantages

### 4.1 Goldman Sachs — Source Density and Proprietary Data

Goldman cited 40+ named sources and had access to proprietary refining margin data, custom oil price scenarios from the Goldman commodities team, and primary executive interview material that DHLM Studio cannot replicate. The Goldman report had 12 stocks vs DHLM's 5, with deeper individual stock coverage on each.

**Gap:** DHLM Studio had 8 named sources. Premium reports per the new Hot Sector Format require minimum 8 — we hit the floor, not the bar. Next Hot Sector should target 12+.

**Mitigation:** Build a process for systematically pulling sell-side research summaries from publicly available transcripts (FactSet StreetAccount, AlphaSense free tier) and citing them by analyst name instead of vague "analysts say."

### 4.2 Bloomberg — Speed and Same-Day Wire Discipline

Bloomberg published their analysis within hours of the ceasefire headline. DHLM Studio published the same day but with a longer review cycle. For institutional readers, the Bloomberg same-day wire is the reference.

**Gap:** DHLM Studio is not competing on raw speed. The competitive position is depth and reader-friendly framing, not wire-speed reaction.

**Mitigation:** Do not try to beat Bloomberg on speed. Lean into the reader-friendly synthesis advantage and accept the 6 to 12 hour publication delay.

### 4.3 Doomberg — Single-Domain Authority and Vertical Depth

Doomberg has been writing about energy for years. The cumulative voice authority and depth of historical knowledge in any single Doomberg piece exceeds what DHLM Studio can produce after a single report. Doomberg subscribers are paying for that domain expertise.

**Gap:** DHLM Studio is positioned as a generalist with sector excursions. We will never be Doomberg in energy specifically.

**Mitigation:** Compete on breadth-with-rigor instead of vertical authority. The DHLM Studio reader gets professional-grade analysis across multiple sectors, not a deep specialist on any one. The pricing reflects this ($99/year vs Doomberg $300/year).

### 4.4 Seeking Alpha — Direct Access to Retail Audience

Seeking Alpha's audience is already on the platform looking for stock ideas. Conversion friction is near zero. DHLM Studio has to acquire the reader, build the email list, then convert to premium. The acquisition cost is meaningfully higher.

**Gap:** Distribution disadvantage that cannot be solved with content quality alone.

**Mitigation:** SEO + Bing Webmaster Tools + AI search engine indexing (already in progress) + the email funnel from PART 18 are the long-term solution. Hot Sector content should be designed to capture organic search traffic on long-tail sector queries that Seeking Alpha already ranks for but covers shallowly.

## 5. Specific Errors and Inaccuracies (Honest Self-Audit)

### 5.1 The "32.7 Percent" Berkshire OXY Stake

DHLM Studio cited Berkshire's OXY economic interest as approximately 32.7 percent. This number is from public 13F filings and is verifiable, but it has fluctuated quarter to quarter and the exact percentage at the report publication date may have been slightly different. The next Hot Sector should pin numbers like this to a specific reporting date.

**Correction approach:** Add explicit "as of [filing date]" to all 13F-derived numbers in future reports.

### 5.2 The 3-2-1 Crack Spread of 40.82

The exact crack spread number was sourced from RBN Energy on April 7, 2026 — the day before publication. The number is real and verifiable, but the report should have explicitly named the date and the exact RBN Energy report or page being cited, not just the publication name.

**Correction approach:** Source citations must include date and specific document reference, not just publication name.

### 5.3 The "Approximately 20 Million Barrels Per Day" Hormuz Number

This is widely cited in EIA literature but the exact number varies by year and methodology. The DHLM Studio report used "approximately" which is appropriate, but the next Hot Sector should anchor with the most recent EIA report year and link directly to the specific document.

**Correction approach:** All EIA / IEA / OPEC data points should cite the specific report and year.

### 5.4 The Refining Margin Sustainability Argument

The DHLM Studio report argued that refining margins can stay elevated even when crude eases because product inventory works down on a delayed cycle. This is directionally correct but the mechanism is more nuanced than the report acknowledged. A peer reviewer with sector expertise could push back on the simplification.

**Correction approach:** Future refining-margin arguments must include specific historical examples of margin persistence after crude reversals (Q3 2014 for example).

## 8. The Lesson That Compounds

The most important pattern from this competitor analysis is that the highest-quality peer publications (Bloomberg, Goldman) win on **source density** and **proprietary data access**, while the lowest-quality peers (Seeking Alpha) win on **distribution**. DHLM Studio cannot win on source density (we are not Goldman) or distribution (we are not Seeking Alpha) in the short term.

What we can win on is **the synthesis layer** — taking the same publicly available data that Bloomberg and Goldman have, organizing it into reader-friendly framing (scenario tables, BEAF scoring, historical parallels), and presenting balanced bull/bear cases that sell-side research is structurally unable to publish.

This is a defensible long-term position. It is also small. Acknowledge the size of the addressable market and price accordingly.
