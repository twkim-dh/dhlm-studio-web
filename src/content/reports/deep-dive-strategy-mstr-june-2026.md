# Strategy: The Bitcoin Standard 2.0 in Practice
## Why MSTR Is Becoming the Operating Model, Not Just the Treasury

Two weeks ago I published a piece called Bitcoin Standard 2.0, describing the emerging three-layer Bitcoin finance stack — Digital Capital at the base, Digital Credit in the middle, and a Digital Yield Interface at the top. That piece was framework-first. This piece is the operating case.

Strategy is the company actively building that architecture in production. Not in a whitepaper. Not in a research thesis. In real time, in front of public-market investors, with a balance sheet, a capital structure, and a quarterly earnings cadence that lets you watch every move the architecture makes.

This is the deep dive on what Strategy actually is in 2026, why I think the company has become structurally more interesting since the 2024-2025 cycle, what the specific risks of the architecture look like now that STRC has scaled past $10 billion, and what the May 26 convertible debt repurchase tells us about the operating model going forward.

There is one specific piece of legal and economic precision I want to put on the table at the start, because most coverage of Strategy gets it wrong. STRC is not a Bitcoin-collateralized note. It is variable-rate perpetual preferred stock with a senior claim on Strategy's residual assets. The economic relationship to Bitcoin is real. The legal collateral relationship is not. That distinction is the difference between owning what Strategy actually issues and owning what casual coverage often implies it issues. I will return to this throughout the piece.

---

## 1. What Strategy stopped being

The simplest mistake retail investors still make about Strategy is treating it as a software company that bought a lot of Bitcoin. That framing was approximately correct in 2020. It is no longer correct in 2026, and the gap between the framing and the reality is large enough that getting it wrong changes the entire analytical setup.

The numbers tell the story directly. Q1 2026 total revenue was $124.3 million, up 11.9 percent year over year — a real, growing software business, but economically small relative to the Bitcoin reserve. As of mid-May 2026, Strategy held 843,738 BTC, acquired for approximately $63.867 billion at an average price of roughly $75,700 per Bitcoin. At then-current prices, the Bitcoin position was worth roughly $65 billion. The software business is a rounding error inside what has structurally become a Bitcoin treasury operating company with a software subsidiary, not a software company with a Bitcoin treasury.

That distinction matters because the stock should no longer be analyzed through software revenue multiples, SaaS margins, or legacy enterprise analytics metrics. The relevant variables are Bitcoin holdings, Bitcoin per share, MNAV premium, preferred equity scale, debt obligations, dividend coverage, cash reserves, capital-market access, and Bitcoin volatility.

This is a capital structure story. And it is one of the most unusual capital structure stories in public markets.

Management has been increasingly explicit about the repositioning. Phong Le and Michael Saylor have, on the most recent earnings calls, started using a different label: "Bitcoin development company." That phrasing matters more than the rebrand from MicroStrategy to Strategy that preceded it. A Bitcoin development company is, in their formulation, an entity that uses corporate capital structure tools to accumulate Bitcoin, issues capital instruments structured around that accumulation, and uses the resulting capital flexibility to fund operations and dividends over time.

That is the Bitcoin Standard 2.0 architecture I described in May, with one specific corporate entity actively operating it.

## 2. Q1 2026 looked terrible on GAAP — and that is not the whole story

The Q1 2026 GAAP picture looks bad on the surface and considerably more interesting underneath it.

The bad part first. Operating loss was $14.47 billion, driven almost entirely by a $14.46 billion unrealized loss on digital assets. Net loss was $12.54 billion. Net loss attributable to common stockholders was $12.77 billion. Those are enormous numbers, and they will dominate any superficial read of the quarter.

The numbers are mostly accounting noise. Under the fair-value accounting regime Strategy now uses, Bitcoin price movements flow directly through the income statement each quarter. Bitcoin fell more than 20 percent in Q1, and that decline showed up as a non-cash mark-to-market loss on the digital asset holdings. By early Q2, $8.3 billion of that unrealized loss had already reversed as prices rebounded.

This is not a footnote. It is a permanent feature of the equity. Investors who analyze Strategy as if quarterly GAAP net income reflects normal operating earnings will misread the company over and over again.

The better analytical approach separates five things: accounting noise from Bitcoin mark-to-market, capital-raising activity, Bitcoin accumulation, Bitcoin per share accretion, and dividend and debt coverage. The operating model lives in those five categories, not in the GAAP headline.

Underneath the accounting noise, the capital activity was substantial. Through early May 2026, Strategy raised $11.68 billion year to date, split roughly between common equity and preferred (primarily STRC). No convertible debt was issued during this window — a meaningful break from the 2021-2024 playbook. Preferred equity outstanding climbed past $13.5 billion. Long-term debt remained around $8.2 billion entering the quarter and was reduced to $6.7 billion through the May 26 repurchase (more on that below).

The company's Bitcoin position grew through aggressive accumulation. Q1 added roughly 89,600 BTC for approximately $5.5 billion — the second-largest quarterly purchase in company history, executed while Bitcoin fell more than 20 percent. Q2-to-date added another 56,235 BTC at an average price of $73,400 for $4.1 billion. Bitcoin per share rose 18 percent year over year. Year-to-date BTC yield reached 9.4 percent, compared to 22.8 percent for full-year 2025.

The architecture is doing what it was designed to do. The Q1 GAAP loss is a symptom of the architecture, not a sign that it is failing.

## 3. The real story is the capital machine

The mechanics of the Strategy model are simple to describe and complicated to operate.

The company issues securities — common equity, preferred equity, convertible debt. It uses the proceeds to buy Bitcoin or manage existing liabilities. If the market values Strategy at a premium to its underlying Bitcoin holdings (MNAV greater than 1.0x), common-stock issuance can be accretive to Bitcoin per share. If Bitcoin appreciates faster than the cost of capital across the stack, the structure compounds. If the premium collapses or Bitcoin enters a prolonged drawdown, the structure becomes much harder to operate.

That is the entire machine. And the most important internal metric is MNAV.

Management's framing on the Q1 call was that the breakeven MNAV threshold for issuing equity to buy Bitcoin has moved above 1.0x as debt and preferred equity have grown. They described approximately 1.22x as an important threshold: above that, common equity issuance to buy Bitcoin is accretive; below that, selling Bitcoin or using other capital tools may be more accretive than issuing equity.

The current reading is approximately 1.27x. That sits above the 1.22x threshold but with less buffer than the model would prefer to operate with at scale.

The implication of this framing is more interesting than the number itself. Strategy is no longer operating as a "buy Bitcoin forever with equity and debt" vehicle. It is operating as a dynamic capital allocation platform that can deploy multiple tools — equity issuance, preferred issuance, debt issuance, debt repurchase, and even Bitcoin sales — based on which is most accretive at any given moment.

That flexibility makes the business more sophisticated. It also makes it more complex, and more dependent on management execution under pressure.

## 4. STRC is the most important product in the capital stack — and the most commonly misunderstood

The single most important capital-structure development inside Strategy over the last twelve months is STRC, also known as Stretch.

STRC is Strategy's variable-rate perpetual preferred stock. It pays an 11.50 percent annualized dividend, distributed monthly in cash, with the dividend rate adjusted monthly to encourage trading around the $100 stated amount. Q1 materials reported STRC scaled to $8.5 billion in just nine months. As of the most recent disclosure, STRC notional value reached approximately $10.49 billion. STRC trades with roughly $375 million in daily volume — the highest liquidity of any tradable preferred security globally, with management citing 25x the liquidity of the next-largest bank preferred.

The combination of yield, liquidity, and indirect Bitcoin exposure has attracted what management describes as roughly 3 million retail beneficiaries plus, unsolicited, corporate treasurers allocating working capital to the instrument. Saylor's stated ambition is to scale that base from millions to tens of millions to hundreds of millions of beneficiaries.

Here is the part that most coverage gets wrong, and that investors most need to understand precisely.

**STRC is not Bitcoin. It is not collateralized by Bitcoin. It is not a money-market fund. It is not a bank deposit. It is not FDIC insured.**

Strategy explicitly states in its preferred securities disclosures that the preferred shares are not collateralized by the company's Bitcoin holdings. Holders of STRC have a preferred claim on Strategy's residual corporate assets after senior creditors. The economic relationship to Bitcoin is real — Strategy's ability to fund STRC dividends depends fundamentally on the value of its Bitcoin reserve and its access to capital markets — but the legal collateral relationship is not what casual coverage often implies.

This is not an academic distinction. It determines what investors actually own. STRC holders own a high-yielding perpetual preferred security issued by a corporate entity whose primary asset is Bitcoin and whose dividend coverage depends on continued favorable Bitcoin economics and capital-market access. That is meaningfully different from owning Bitcoin-collateralized debt.

The financial mechanics of STRC are where the architecture gets interesting. Management's framing on the Q1 call was that the BTC breakeven annualized return — the level of Bitcoin appreciation Strategy needs to fully cover STRC dividend obligations under the current model — is approximately 2.27 percent. They argued that if Bitcoin appreciates above that level over time, Strategy can fund preferred dividends indefinitely under current assumptions. They also stated that if Bitcoin appreciates 0 percent annually from here, current cash reserves imply many years of dividend coverage.

That 2.27 percent figure is a powerful number. Bitcoin's rolling 30-day volatility is 34 percent. Its trailing-one-year volatility approaches 40 percent. The expected annualized return Saylor cites is closer to 39 percent. The margin between expected return and breakeven is wide.

But the number is a model output, not a guarantee. It depends on assumptions about Bitcoin price behavior, preferred issuance pace, dividend obligations, cash reserves, liability management, market access, MNAV stability, and future capital raising. Each of those assumptions can move against the company under stress, and the 2.27 percent calculation does not survive every scenario.

Read the number as a window into how management thinks about the structure. Do not read it as proof that the structure is risk-free.

## 5. The May 26 debt repurchase reveals the new operating mode

The most important post-Q1 update is Strategy's May 26 convertible debt repurchase, and it is the cleanest evidence to date that the company is operating as a capital structure optimizer rather than a static Bitcoin accumulator.

On May 26, Strategy announced it completed the repurchase of $1.5 billion aggregate principal amount of its 2029 convertible notes for approximately $1.38 billion in cash — roughly an 8 percent discount to par. The transaction reduced aggregate convertible notes outstanding from $8.2 billion to $6.7 billion. Management reported that the move generated BTC Yield of 0.7 percent, BTC Gain of 4,391 BTC, and BTC dollar gain of $333 million.

This is exactly the kind of move investors should expect from the new Strategy.

It reduces debt. It lowers future dilution risk from the convertible conversion feature. It uses cash reserves opportunistically when the market offers an 8 percent discount on the company's own paper. And critically, it demonstrates that management is no longer simply buying Bitcoin mechanically with whatever capital is available — they are now actively managing liabilities and optimizing the capital structure across multiple dimensions.

This is the operational shift that matters most for the forward thesis. Strategy in 2026 is no longer best understood as a one-directional Bitcoin accumulator. It is a multi-directional capital allocator that can choose between:

— Buying Bitcoin with new common equity issuance (when MNAV is above the accretive threshold)
— Buying Bitcoin with preferred equity issuance (when preferred markets are receptive)
— Buying Bitcoin with convertible debt (when debt markets are favorable)
— Repurchasing debt at discounts (when company paper trades below par)
— Holding cash reserves to maintain dividend coverage flexibility
— Selling Bitcoin or using Bitcoin derivatives when alternatives are less accretive

The May 26 repurchase is the first publicly visible example of the company executing the fourth option at material scale. It will not be the last. The architecture now includes a real toolkit, and management has demonstrated they are willing to use the full toolkit.

## 6. The three-layer framework, mapped to Strategy with precision

The Bitcoin Standard 2.0 framework maps to Strategy's capital stack — but with a precision adjustment I want to make explicit, because the framework gets distorted when applied loosely.

**Layer 1 — Digital Capital.** Strategy's Bitcoin holdings themselves. 843,738 BTC at an average cost of roughly $75,700 per coin, valued at approximately $65 billion at mid-May 2026 prices. The base layer of the architecture, owned outright by the operating company, marked to fair value, and serving as the economic substrate for everything above it.

**Layer 2 — Digital Credit.** The preferred equity stack, dominated by STRC ($10+ billion notional and growing) and supplemented by the other listed preferred series (STRF, STRD, STRK). Plus convertible debt now reduced to $6.7 billion through the May 26 repurchase. These instruments are economically backed by Strategy's Bitcoin-heavy balance sheet and operating model, even though they are not legally collateralized by Bitcoin. STRC alone is now the largest tradable preferred security in the world by liquidity.

**Layer 3 — Digital Yield Interface.** This is the precision adjustment. I had originally written about this layer as "Digital Money & Yield," which understates the legal and structural distinction between what Strategy actually issues and what stablecoins or tokenized Treasuries represent. STRC is not money. It is not a money-market equivalent. The official risk language is explicit on that point.

A more accurate description of Layer 3 is a Digital Yield Interface — a set of monthly cash distributions, preferred instruments, and capital-market products designed to convert Bitcoin's long-term appreciation thesis into current income that public-market investors can hold and trade. That is what STRC is, and it is what makes the Strategy stack genuinely innovative. It just is not money, and it should not be marketed as money.

The three layers are not independent. They interact through the company's capital structure, the MNAV ratio, the BTC yield calculation, and the dividend obligations on the preferred stack. When Layer 1 (Bitcoin) appreciates, Layer 2 (preferred coverage) becomes structurally safer and Layer 3 (yield product attractiveness) gets more competitive against bank preferreds. When Layer 1 declines, the pressure works in reverse — Layer 2 dividend coverage tightens, Layer 3 distribution economics get harder, and the company's capital allocation choices narrow.

That coupling is the structural feature investors most need to understand. Strategy is not three businesses. It is one architecture with three exit ramps for different classes of capital, all pivoting on the same underlying Bitcoin position.

## 7. The five-year outperformance versus Bitcoin itself

Strategy has outperformed Bitcoin itself over the last five years by a wide margin. Five-year returns through mid-May 2026 are approximately 262 percent for Strategy versus approximately 79 percent for Bitcoin.

That outperformance is the entire empirical argument for the leveraged architecture. If Strategy simply matched Bitcoin's return — or underperformed it — there would be no operating case for the structure. The thesis only works if the accretion mechanism compounds at a rate that beats the underlying asset over multi-year periods.

The historical record says it has worked. The forward question is whether the conditions that enabled it continue to hold.

The accretion mechanism requires three things simultaneously. First, MNAV at a premium that allows common-stock issuance to be accretive. Second, accessible debt and preferred capital markets that provide the leverage amplifying Bitcoin exposure beyond pure equity-funded accumulation. Third, Bitcoin appreciation at rates that justify the capital cost of the leverage.

All three conditions held through 2020-2025. In 2026, two of them are still holding. MNAV at 1.27 sits above the 1.22 accretive threshold management has identified. Capital markets remain accessible at scale through STRC. The third condition is operating under more cyclical stress — Bitcoin's 20+ percent Q1 drawdown tested the architecture's resilience in a way the 2024 bull market did not.

The question for the next twelve to twenty-four months is whether the architecture can deliver outperformance versus Bitcoin during a period of greater cyclical volatility than the 2024-2025 setup provided. The Q1 results, taken in context with the May 26 debt repurchase and continued accumulation at $73,400 average prices, suggest the answer remains yes for now. The architecture is being managed more actively, more sophisticated, and with more capital structure tools than at any previous point.

The historical track record is real. The forward path is not guaranteed.

## 8. The bear cases that matter

The bullish architecture description is real, and so are the risks. Five matter most.

**The reflexive structure works in reverse during sustained Bitcoin drawdowns.** The Q1 2026 20+ percent drawdown tested the architecture and the architecture held. A 50+ percent drawdown sustained over a year or longer would test it differently. STRC dividend coverage at a 2.27 percent BTC breakeven ARR looks comfortable when Bitcoin is appreciating. It looks much less comfortable when Bitcoin is declining over an extended period. The thesis is multi-decade in management's framing. The architecture needs to survive multi-year stress tests inside that horizon, including ones it has not yet faced.

**MNAV compression breaks the accretion machine.** Current 1.27 MNAV sits modestly above the 1.22 threshold management identifies as the accretive boundary. If MNAV compresses toward or below that level — through Bitcoin appreciation outpacing equity price, through dilution accelerating faster than accumulation, or through a sustained equity premium contraction — the accretion mechanism stalls. Common-stock issuance becomes dilutive rather than accretive, and the company faces real strategic choices between slowing accumulation, accepting dilution, selling Bitcoin to fund dividends, or pivoting toward more debt repurchases and capital structure optimization.

**STRC scaling creates real ongoing obligations.** $10+ billion in perpetual preferred at 11.5 percent dividends translates to roughly $1.15 billion per year in dividend obligations at full notional. If Bitcoin enters a sustained drawdown and capital markets tighten simultaneously, those dividend obligations have to be paid regardless. The 11.5 percent yield is what makes STRC attractive to buyers. It is also what makes the obligations meaningful for the issuer. STRC holders should remember that they own a preferred claim on residual corporate assets, not a Bitcoin-collateralized note — the dividend coverage depends on continued favorable economics, not on collateral seizure rights.

**The model depends on continued capital-market access.** Strategy's architecture works because it can continue raising capital across multiple instruments. If a stress scenario impairs that access — through a credit event, regulatory change, accounting shift, or sustained Bitcoin decline that reprices the entire corporate credit profile — the company's flexibility narrows quickly. Capital-market access is not a constant. It is conditional on conditions that have generally held through the 2020-2026 cycle and may not hold under all future conditions.

**Bitcoin treasury company competition compresses the architectural premium.** Strategy is no longer the only listed Bitcoin treasury company. The 2024-2026 cycle produced a cohort of corporate Bitcoin accumulation vehicles, several of which are now meaningful enough to compete for the same retail and institutional capital that has historically flowed to MSTR and STRC. If the architecture works, it gets copied. If it gets copied at scale, the architectural premium Strategy currently holds compresses, and some portion of the accretion mechanism flows to peers rather than concentrating in Strategy's stack.

Owning the long thesis responsibly means owning all five of these risks. The architecture is real. The forward path is, like every capital-structure innovation in financial history, conditional.

## 9. What U.S. investors should actually watch

The practical takeaway is a positioning framework, not a single recommendation. And the most important point is that the three Strategy-related exposures are not interchangeable.

**Bitcoin direct exposure** — through spot, ETFs (IBIT, FBTC, the broader complex), or direct holdings — is pure asset exposure. Bitcoin's volatility, with no architectural overlay. For investors who want Bitcoin without taking a view on Strategy's capital structure, this is the appropriate vehicle.

**Strategy common equity (MSTR)** — gives leveraged exposure to Bitcoin's appreciation through the MNAV premium and the accretion mechanism. The five-year track record of outperformance versus Bitcoin is the empirical argument. The five risks above are the structural ones. For investors who want to take a view that the architecture continues to compound, this is the vehicle.

**Strategy preferred equity (STRC, STRF, STRD, STRK)** — gives yield-bearing exposure to Strategy's corporate credit profile, economically influenced by Bitcoin but legally a senior claim on the company's residual assets rather than Bitcoin-collateralized debt. The 11.5 percent STRC yield is the most attractive headline. The credit risk is the company's ability to maintain dividend coverage through Bitcoin cycles and continued capital-market access. For investors who want yield exposure with indirect Bitcoin economic linkage, this is the vehicle.

The most important indicators for monitoring the position over time are: total BTC held, Bitcoin per share, MNAV ratio, STRC notional value, STRC trading level versus the $100 stated amount, STRC dividend rate, cash reserve balance, convertible debt outstanding, preferred dividend obligations, Bitcoin sales or derivative usage, debt repurchases, new preferred issuance, and market appetite for digital credit products.

Investors who collapse these into one undifferentiated category are missing the architecture. Investors who track them carefully see the operating model in real time.

## Closing

I wrote Bitcoin Standard 2.0 two weeks ago as a framework piece. Strategy is the deep dive because the company is the framework in operation.

The architecture is real. The five-year outperformance versus Bitcoin is the empirical argument that the architecture compounds. The Q1 2026 results — terrible on the GAAP surface, structurally informative underneath — show that the model can absorb cyclical Bitcoin stress and continue executing capital activity at scale. STRC has become the central instrument in the stack, scaled to $10+ billion in nine months, and is now a meaningful part of the U.S. preferred-equity market. The May 26 convertible debt repurchase demonstrated that management is now actively optimizing liabilities, not merely accumulating Bitcoin.

The single legal precision I want to leave with: STRC is not Bitcoin-collateralized. The economic linkage is real. The legal collateral relationship is not. Investors should own that distinction precisely before owning the preferred securities.

The forward question is whether the conditions that enabled the architecture to work through 2020-2025 — premium MNAV above 1.22x, accessible capital markets, Bitcoin appreciation at sufficient rates — continue to hold through a more cyclically volatile period. The architecture is built to survive that volatility. The track record suggests it has. The forward catalysts that matter most are MNAV stability, STRC scaling without dividend strain, continued Bitcoin appreciation at rates that justify the leveraged capital cost, and continued capital-market access across instruments.

If I had to compress this into one sentence:

**Bitcoin is the asset. Strategy is the machine. STRC is the yield interface. The opportunity is that the machine keeps compounding. The risk is that the machine now has obligations Bitcoin itself does not.**

The Q2 2026 numbers, when they arrive, will be the first real test of the architecture post-Q1 stress. MNAV, STRC trading levels, and the dividend coverage commentary on the next earnings call are the data points that matter most. The May 26 debt repurchase already gave us one data point that the new operating mode is working as designed. The next earnings call will tell us whether it continues to work as the architecture scales.

This is not a buy recommendation. It is a structural read on the most important Bitcoin treasury company in public markets, the architecture it has built, and the precision investors need to bring to owning any part of the capital stack.
