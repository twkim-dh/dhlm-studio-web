# Quantum Computing 101 — Part 12

## The Quantum Investing Watchlist: Companies, Layers, and Signals Worth Tracking

> **About this series — and a critical note on this final part.** This is an educational resource. The lists below are a **watchlist, not a buy list** — a map of where companies sit in the quantum industry and which signals to track, *not* a set of recommendations. Many companies named here are unprofitable; some may fail technically; some hold good technology at expensive prices. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any security.
>
> **Disclosure:** The author holds a position in IonQ (IONQ), one of the companies referenced below. This is disclosed in the interest of transparency and does not constitute a recommendation.

This is the final installment of the series. We have covered the technical approaches, applications, the security market, and the investing framework. Now it is time to build a watchlist a U.S. reader can actually track.

In one middle-school sentence: **A quantum watchlist is not a "list of the coolest companies." It is a map of who could make money in which layer as the quantum industry grows.**

Quantum computing is still an early industry, so picking a single stock is risky. It helps to view companies in five groups: **large platform companies, public quantum pure-plays, key private companies, quantum-security and PQC companies, and supply-chain / semiconductor / infrastructure companies.** Seen together, the whole industry comes into much sharper focus.

---

## 1. Large Platform Companies: Holding Quantum as a "Long-Term Call Option"

This group is not driven by quantum computing alone, but it may hold the strongest distribution channels when quantum commercializes. Representative companies: **IBM, Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Honeywell.**

**IBM** is the representative platform for superconducting quantum computing, targeting up to 360 qubits and 7,500-gate circuits on Nighthawk-based systems in 2026, expanding to 10,000 gates in 2027 and 15,000 in 2028 — growing *real circuit-execution capability*, not just qubit count. In May 2026, IBM was selected for the largest single allocation in the U.S. Department of Commerce's CHIPS Act quantum package: **$1 billion** to establish **Anderon**, described as America's first pure-play quantum chip foundry in Albany, New York (IBM is matching with $1 billion of its own cash). So IBM links hardware, software, cloud, *and* manufacturing infrastructure.

> *An educational perspective:* IBM is not a pure-play, but one of the most institutionalized quantum platforms — lower risk, but less stock leverage to quantum success than a pure-play.

**Alphabet / Google** is one of the most important research companies in superconducting quantum computing (Willow, error correction, quantum-advantage work). Its strength is viewing quantum alongside AI, cloud, data centers, and research infrastructure.

> *An educational perspective:* Not a stock you buy for quantum alone, but a strong technology platform if AI and quantum research combine long-term.

**Microsoft** pursues the high-risk, high-reward topological-qubit approach while seeking to control the deployment layer via Azure Quantum, Azure Quantum Elements, AI, HPC, and an enterprise customer base. Its hardware thesis carries large scientific-verification risk, but its cloud and software layers are already strong.

> *An educational perspective:* Microsoft is an AI/cloud/enterprise platform plus a quantum long-term option — not a pure-play.

**Amazon** provides multi-hardware quantum cloud access via Amazon Braket, acting as a distribution platform connecting many hardware types to AWS customers rather than betting on one modality.

> *An educational perspective:* Amazon is the cloud layer that lets customers use whichever hardware wins.

**Honeywell** is an industrial platform deeply tied to Quantinuum (formed from Honeywell Quantum Solutions and Cambridge Quantum), strong in trapped-ion hardware and quantum software. Quantinuum entered the public market via a traditional IPO in 2026, opening at $68 (above its $60 IPO price) for a roughly **$17.63 billion** valuation at the open; its 2025 revenue was about $30.9 million with a net loss of about $192.6 million — an early, high-loss stage.

> *An educational perspective:* Honeywell is not a direct pure-play but connects to a high-performance trapped-ion platform through Quantinuum.

## 2. Public Quantum Pure-Plays: Biggest Upside, Biggest Volatility

More direct exposure to the quantum industry — and far more risk. Representative companies: **IonQ, Rigetti, D-Wave, Quantinuum.**

**IonQ** is one of the most important U.S.-listed pure-plays, built on trapped-ion quantum computing. Its strengths: trapped-ion accuracy and connectivity; relatively fast-growing commercial revenue; and expansion into quantum networking, sensing, security, and government projects. IonQ reported Q1 2026 revenue of $64.7 million (+755% YoY) and raised full-year 2026 guidance to **$260–270 million**, with remaining performance obligations of $470 million and $3.1 billion in cash and investments — a larger revenue base than D-Wave or Rigetti.

> *An educational perspective:* IonQ has among the highest commercial visibility of the pure-plays, but valuation, roadmap, long-term profitability, and modality-competition risks are large. *(See disclosure above.)*

**Rigetti** is a superconducting pure-play. If IBM and Google are the large platforms, Rigetti is more directly exposed to the superconducting approach — higher stock leverage than IBM/Google, but far larger capital and technology risk. Its revenue base is still very small in absolute terms despite high growth rates, and it received $100 million in the 2026 CHIPS quantum package. Some analysts have highlighted Rigetti as a quantum pick, citing the superconducting approach and federal funding, but technology-timeline and revenue-scale risks remain large.

> *An educational perspective:* Rigetti is a superconducting pure-play — bigger stock leverage than IBM/Google, but bigger capital and technology risk.

**D-Wave Quantum** is the representative quantum-annealing company — optimization-specialized rather than general-purpose — while also pursuing a gate-model roadmap. It received $100 million in the 2026 CHIPS package. Its revenue is still small and variable.

> *An educational perspective:* D-Wave is best seen as an "optimization-specialized commercial quantum company plus a long-term gate-model option" — do not compare it on universal-computer terms alone.

**Quantinuum** is a full-stack quantum company combining trapped-ion hardware, software, cybersecurity, quantum random numbers, and enterprise applications. Its 2026 IPO raised the sector's credibility, and it received $100 million in the CHIPS package. But readers must stay cold-eyed: it listed at a roughly $17.63 billion open-valuation while 2025 revenue was $30.9 million and net loss $192.6 million — meaning expectations are already heavily priced in.

> *An educational perspective:* Technically strong full-stack company, but the gap between valuation and current financials must be tracked continuously.

## 3. Key Private Companies: Shaping the Industry from Outside Public Markets

Hard for U.S. retail to buy directly, but essential to track. Several received CHIPS Act awards in May 2026.

**PsiQuantum** — the representative photonic company, targeting a large-scale fault-tolerant machine from the start, tied to manufacturing partners like GlobalFoundries; $100 million CHIPS award. *Track IPO, partnerships, government funding, and foundry-capacity news.* The photonic moonshot.

**Atom Computing** — the representative U.S. neutral-atom company; its Microsoft collaboration links it to the Azure Quantum ecosystem; $100 million CHIPS award. Shows whether neutral atom can enter cloud and enterprise quantum workflows.

**QuEra** — important for its neutral-atom programmable array and AWS Braket accessibility, with Harvard–MIT research roots and strength in analog quantum simulation. A software-accessible neutral-atom hardware company.

**Pasqal** — Europe's representative neutral-atom company, important within EuroHPC and European quantum infrastructure (and pursuing a public listing via SPAC). Low direct access for U.S. readers, but important for reading European quantum policy.

**Diraq** — the representative silicon-spin technology company, emphasizing modified-silicon-transistor design and CMOS compatibility; included in the 2026 CHIPS package ($38 million). Shows whether quantum computing can extend onto existing semiconductor processes.

**Quantum Motion** — a UK-based silicon quantum-computing company aiming at a data-center-friendly silicon system. Shows whether silicon spin can take commercial-infrastructure form.

**Xanadu** — holds photonic quantum hardware *and* the PennyLane software ecosystem; sits at the intersection of the photonic approach and quantum software.

**Classiq** — focuses on quantum software, circuit synthesis, and high-level algorithm-design automation. As hardware diversifies, a software-abstraction layer like this can grow more valuable.

## 4. Quantum-Security and PQC Watchlist: The Layer Where Money May Flow First

Post-quantum cryptography is not a market that begins after quantum computers are finished — it is a security transition already underway. NIST has finalized ML-KEM, ML-DSA, and SLH-DSA as primary PQC standards (with HQC added later as a backup), and says they are ready for use now.

The key point: **PQC can see budgets before quantum hardware matures.**

**Cloudflare** — web traffic, edge security, TLS, Zero Trust; PQC transition may show up first at the internet edge.

**Palo Alto Networks** — enterprise network security, SASE, cloud security, firewall infrastructure.

**Zscaler** — Zero Trust access and cloud security gateways, where PQC-ready traffic management matters.

**CrowdStrike** — endpoint, identity, and cloud-workload security, connected to update signing, authentication, and long-term data protection.

**Okta** — identity is central to crypto transition; login, authentication, and access control are all part of quantum-safe migration.

**DigiCert / Sectigo** — the certificate ecosystem is a key PQC bottleneck; changing certificates reshapes internet trust.

**Thales / Entrust / IBM Security** — HSMs, key management, cryptographic inventory, and enterprise security infrastructure.

> *An educational perspective:* The PQC watchlist is closer to "cybersecurity infrastructure" than "quantum stocks." Before the quantum threat materializes, governments and enterprises must find and replace cryptographic assets.

## 5. Supply-Chain / Semiconductor / Infrastructure Watchlist: The "Picks and Shovels"

As the industry grows, hardware makers are not the only winners — cooling, optics, photonics, semiconductor manufacturing, control electronics, test equipment, and data-center infrastructure all matter.

**GlobalFoundries** — selected for a **$375 million** CHIPS award to establish Quantum Technology Solutions, a quantum foundry division spanning five qubit modalities (with the government taking ~1% equity); a core piece of U.S. domestic quantum manufacturing alongside IBM's Anderon. Not a pure-play, but important for quantum-foundry capacity, silicon photonics, and specialty manufacturing.

**Intel** — important in silicon-spin quantum computing and advanced semiconductor manufacturing; not a pure-play, but its manufacturing capability could be a large asset if silicon spin scales.

**Coherent / Lumentum / Hamamatsu and optics/laser companies** — trapped-ion, neutral-atom, and photonic approaches depend on lasers and optics; as quantum hardware grows, high-quality optical components and the laser supply chain matter.

**Bluefors / Oxford Instruments and cryogenic-equipment companies** — superconducting and silicon-spin systems need cryogenics; dilution refrigerators and cryogenic control are essential infrastructure.

**Keysight / Tektronix / Zurich Instruments-type companies** — quantum systems need precise control and measurement: control electronics, microwave electronics, signal generators, and test equipment.

**NVIDIA** — not a quantum-hardware company, but potentially important in quantum simulation, hybrid quantum-classical computing, and AI/HPC infrastructure; the more quantum combines with HPC, the more it may connect to NVIDIA's ecosystem.

> *An educational perspective:* Supply-chain companies are less flashy than pure-plays but can play the picks-and-shovels role in industry growth.

## 6. Dividing the Watchlist by Investing Purpose

Readers should hold different watchlists depending on goals. *(Illustrative groupings, not recommendations.)*

**Conservative** — IBM, Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Honeywell: hold quantum upside as a long-term option, keep core-business stability, avoid pure-play volatility.

**Growth** — IonQ, Quantinuum, Rigetti, D-Wave, plus IBM and Microsoft: pure-play exposure cushioned by large platforms; track government support and revenue growth together.

**Aggressive** — IonQ, Rigetti, D-Wave, Quantinuum, plus newly public names like Infleqtion: maximize sector beta; accept high volatility and the possibility of 50%+ drawdowns.

**Cybersecurity-focused** — Cloudflare, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler, CrowdStrike, Okta, Thales, Entrust, IBM Security: capture PQC-migration budgets — real demand *before* quantum hardware succeeds.

**Infrastructure-focused** — GlobalFoundries, Intel, Coherent, Lumentum, cooling/optics/control-equipment makers, NVIDIA: picks-and-shovels exposure that reduces single-modality-winner risk.

## 7. Key Monitoring Metrics

Once a watchlist exists, track the signals.

**Hardware:** physical qubits, logical qubits, two-qubit fidelity, error rate, coherence time, circuit depth, gate count, interconnect progress, error-correction milestones.

**Commercial:** revenue, bookings, backlog, remaining performance obligations, gross margin, customer count, cloud usage, government contracts.

**Financial:** cash balance, quarterly burn, dilution, debt, preferred equity, runway, capital-raise terms.

**Policy:** CHIPS Act funding, DARPA programs, DOE programs, NIST standards, defense contracts, export controls.

**Security:** PQC adoption, cryptographic-inventory tools, certificate migration, HSM upgrades, TLS/PQC integration, government procurement requirements.

## 8. The Most Important Warning: A Watchlist Is Not a Buy List

This must be emphasized. **A watchlist is not a buy list — it is an observation list.**

Quantum computing may be a huge future market, but many companies are still losing money. Some may fail technically. Some may burn too much capital before commercialization. Some may hold good technology yet trade at too-high prices.

McKinsey projects quantum-computing revenue could grow from about $4 billion in 2024 to as much as $72 billion by 2035 — but a large industry opening does not make every quantum stock a good investment. Remember this line:

> **A great technology can still be a bad stock at the wrong price.**

## 10. Series Conclusion

Quantum computing is not a finished industry — but it is no longer a mere lab curiosity. The U.S. government treats quantum as a national-security technology; NIST has finalized PQC standards; the Department of Commerce announced $2.013 billion in quantum incentives; large platforms (IBM, Google, Microsoft, Amazon) are linking quantum to AI, cloud, and HPC; and pure-plays (IonQ, Rigetti, D-Wave, Quantinuum) are raising capital in public markets and attempting commercialization.

Readers should be excited *and* cold-eyed. Quantum computing could become a very large market — but it is also a very difficult one. The best approach is not single-stock conviction but **layer-by-layer observation:**

- Who builds the best qubits?
- Who reduces errors?
- Who wins customers?
- Who controls cloud deployment?
- Who earns money first in the security transition?
- Who gets inside government budgets and industry supply chains?
- Who has the cash and capital structure to survive?

Keep tracking these questions.

## Final Line
