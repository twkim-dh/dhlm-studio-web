## Full Note

The Apple / CXMT story broke on June 26-27, 2026 (Financial Times, Bloomberg). The headline was bearish: Apple wants to buy DRAM from a cheaper Chinese supplier. The actual read is more interesting.

Apple is trying to negotiate a discount from suppliers who just posted record-shattering margins. Micron's Q3 guide for Q4 was ~$50B revenue at ~86% gross margin. In memory — historically one of the most cyclical, commoditized businesses in tech — those are scarcity margins.

The key question isn't whether CXMT can make DRAM. It can. The question is whether the threat is *credible* enough to actually move pricing. And here's where I think the conventional read gets it wrong.

**The political risk makes CXMT a weak card.** CXMT is on the Pentagon's Section 1260H list. The approval Apple needs is slow, politically sensitive, and comes with no guarantee against future escalation. The YMTC precedent (2022, NAND) is instructive: Apple planned to use YMTC in China-market iPhones, then export controls tightened and it walked back to its incumbent vendors.

**The Micron-Anthropic agreement matters.** On June 22, 2026, Micron and Anthropic announced a direct strategic agreement to scale AI infrastructure. An AI lab signing directly with a memory maker is not how commodities behave. It's how strategic resources behave.

My thesis hasn't changed. The AI era made memory demand durable, strategic, and visible. Apple's gambit, if anything, validates the thesis. The companies that make the physical substrate AI runs on may hold the edge longer than the market's current framing suggests.

**What I'm watching:** Micron's Q4 print vs. the ~$50B guide. Whether Washington grants Apple any CXMT clearance — and how narrow. New-fab output timing.
